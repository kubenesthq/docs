# Fixture: this page MUST NOT trip the guard

Prose that sits close to the retired claims without making them. If the guard
fires here it is over-matching, which costs a publish and trains people to
ignore it.

The control plane stores an encrypted cluster bearer token, and the operator
holds its own credentials in the cluster.

A compromised control plane can build a Kubernetes client from what it holds,
which is why the earlier claim was retired.

Most cluster operations are routed through the hub to the in-cluster agent.

The restore drill proves the mechanism with its own proof set, and it reports what it verified —
objects restored and matched, and volume bytes read back.

KubeNest does not sell, require or configure kernel livepatching; enrolling your hosts would mean a
commercial subscription and a credential in the install flow.

## The truths that replaced the retired claims

Volume data is protected by a per-cluster repository password, generated at install and carried in
the cluster kit.

The control plane's database is checkpointed to the bucket nightly, and a security change triggers
its own checkpoint; a recovery loads the newest eligible one.

The control plane has its own certificate authority as the issuer for the control-plane chart, and
that CA travels in the control-plane kit rather than being the host cluster's.

A datastore snapshot is the documented fallback for rebuilding a workload cluster, and nothing from
the dead host's datastore is replayed by the supported recovery.
